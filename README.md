### ⚙️ What I Am Building Now

- **Automated intelligence report engine.** Defined the report set (country, sector, region, ransomware, and executive briefs written for CEO and CISO audiences) and shipped it inside the product codebase rather than writing requirements for someone else to implement. Publishing is automated, so the public report library stays current without anyone maintaining it by hand.
- **Evidence-bound executive briefs.** Rebuilt CEO and CISO brief generation so every assessment resolves back to the verified evidence underneath it. When a model writes intelligence, grounding is the entire problem; this is where it is either solved or quietly skipped.
- **Ransomware and breach monitoring.** Two threat sources ingested every five minutes with cross-source deduplication and alerting into Slack, Telegram and X, plus a separate dark web breach-feed monitor on a fifteen-minute cycle.
- **Ransomware dataset and threat map.** 3,000+ victim records across 68 groups and 98 countries normalised into quarterly datasets, with an interactive D3 world threat map built on top.
- **AI content production tooling.** Next.js and TypeScript on PostgreSQL, driven by a three-stage LLM agent pipeline with DOCX and PDF export, deployed through GitLab CI/CD.
- **Certified training.** Three courses authored and delivered, 142 slides and 21 recorded modules with bilingual assessments, now part of a public security academy.

Alongside that: small tools that do one job, document it properly, and keep their legal boundaries in the code rather than in a disclaimer.

### 🧪 Platform And Vendor Assessment

I evaluate threat intelligence platforms against the requirements they are supposed to serve: what a team actually needs collected, how sources are graded, whether findings carry confidence, and whether the output survives a sceptical read. That comes from owning the intelligence capabilities a platform roadmap had to deliver, and from mapping the vendor landscape into direct, adjacent and out-of-category tiers rather than treating every logo as a competitor.

<details>
<summary><b>🧭 How I work</b></summary>

Method is the part intelligence work usually leaves unstated, so I state mine.

- Requirements come first and drive collection, not the other way round
- Sources are graded for reliability before anything is built on them
- Competing hypotheses are tested rather than the first one confirmed
- Every assessment carries an explicit confidence statement
- Actors and campaigns are modelled against MITRE ATT&CK and the Diamond Model, so findings stay comparable over time instead of being one-off notes

</details>
